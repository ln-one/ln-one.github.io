These are the standards I hold my own work to.

1. **Integrity comes first. My credibility rests on the honesty of my work.**
2. AI is a tool. I own my work and my choices, accept full responsibility, and am prepared to bear the consequences.
3. I do not pursue zoo-style metaheuristic optimization: wrapping familiar search operators in animal-behavior metaphors, naming the combination after a species, and presenting it as a new optimizer.
4. I do not add complexity for an appearance of sophistication. A mechanism needs to outperform a simple alternative or baseline, or offer another clear benefit.
5. I minimize additional hyperparameters and keep test results out of method design and configuration selection. Expanding the tuning space, repeatedly selecting against test results, and reporting the selected gains as evidence of effectiveness is data leakage—whether deliberate or inadvertent.
6. I aim for clear, concise language, avoiding titles such as “An Intelligent Electrical Load Forecasting System Based on Deep Learning and a Transformer with Multi-Head Self-Attention” (a Transformer-based electrical load forecasting system).
7. I avoid self-praise and empty claims such as “significantly better,” “the first,” or “state of the art.”
